## 1. Purpose and claim structure

Primary question: does gene-guided repair (C3L) improve solver-executed accuracy over generic execution-feedback repair (C3E) when both start from the identical initial program?

The mechanism is examined in three parts, each with its own evidence:

1. **Detection.** Gene predicates flag errors that execution feedback does not.
2. **Diagnosis and prescription.** With the same trigger and the same number of calls, the gene-specific diagnosis and repair instruction add accuracy beyond an equal-length generic OR review checklist.
3. **Delivery.** The same rules delivered as executable checks with targeted repair, compared with the same rules written into the prompt.

Properties of the gene *form* (versioning, scope, provenance, auditability) are documented with artifacts (traces, hashes, per-gene lifecycle statistics), not inferred from accuracy differences. The paper does not claim that form and knowledge are each necessary.

**Development results are in-sample.** On dev55, 7 of the 8 problems solved only by C3L were source problems of the gene that triggered the repair. On the 27 source problems C0/C3E/C3L solved 6/7/14; on the 28 non-source problems 21/21/22. Development results are reported as a development record, not as effect evidence or a basis for power.

## 3. Models and conditions

- Primary model qwen3:8b (Ollama `qwen3:8b`, digest 500a1f067a9f782620b40bee6f7b0c89e17ae61f686b92c24933e4ca4b2b8b41, registry entry `qwen3-8b.main`), temperature 0, seed 1, cold load per problem. Second open-weight model gpt-oss:20b (reasoning "low", output limit 6,000 tokens: in the dev rehearsal 93 calls had a median of 831 and a maximum of 4,749 output tokens) runs C0, C1, C2, C3, C3E, C3L. Known server defect, rule fixed before the test (2026-10-06): the inference server (Ollama 0.33.0, pinned for both models) sometimes rejects a gpt-oss response with HTTP 500 "error parsing tool call" when the model writes its program into the tool-call channel; the same request fails again deterministically. In the dev rehearsal this hit 3 of 120 round-0 calls (two problems; the same problem succeeded under one condition and failed under others). Such a unit is recorded as `server_refused` with the error text, counted as not executed (wrong) in every gpt-oss analysis, and any repair unit that depends on its round 0 is recorded the same way; the number and problem ids are reported per condition, and every gpt-oss result is also reported excluding the refused problems (complete-case) next to the main rule. The expected loss is small: about 2.5% of round-0 calls in the rehearsal, none of 93 calls in repair rounds; a round-0 refusal removes the problem from the paired comparison without bias, and a repair-round refusal would count against the condition that made the call, more often C3L. No request is reworded and the server is not upgraded, which would change the pinned identity of both models.
- Existing conditions as in the TODO: C0, C1, C2, C3, C3E, C3L, C2E, and the measured anchors ORLM and LLMOPT (same test problems and scorer; each with its official prompt and decoding). The anchors run through a separate inference server (llama.cpp) whose decoding settings are fixed after their own dev rehearsal; they are registered in a superseding freeze manifest before their test run and are not part of the first manifest. C1 is the header and a prefix of a pool of generic modelling advice, matched in tokens to the dev mean of the C3 system prompts (62 of 77 entries, ratio 1.003 for qwen3:8b and 1.021 in the second model's encoding, so both models use the same C1); pool entries that restate gene wording were replaced before matching. A same-model comparison with an experience-library method (AlphaOPT) and prompt paraphrase variants are not run in this revision; both are stated as limitations. The three-stage agent baseline (ORA) is not run in this revision (author decision 2026-10-03); the generate–execute–debug comparison is C3E, with C3A and C0R+E as further controls, and the omission is stated in the limitations.
- Library: it1 (Section 6.1). C3L activation is the lexicon v2 status quo (only `logic_via_indicators` is scoped; other genes are always active and self-gate through their predicates).
- All repair conditions reuse the byte-identical C0 round-0 response and allow at most 2 repair rounds. No cross-condition reuse after round 0; identical requests are used only as determinism checks.
- No per-problem total token cap. Every condition has the same per-call limits (num_predict 6,000, num_ctx 32,768) and repair conditions the same round limit (2), which bound each unit; matching is by trigger, rounds and calls, not by tokens. Tokens and calls per problem are reported for every condition, split into the share of problems that trigger repair and the tokens per triggered problem. Reason (checked on dev55 by replaying the runner's cap rule, under which a call is made only while the unit's total is below the cap): a cap at the 90th or 95th percentile of C3L (6,971 and 7,963 server-reported tokens; bootstrap 95% CI of the 90th percentile 4,347–8,032) stops no call in C3L or C3E and changes no outcome, because units pass it only during their last call; a cap low enough to bind (the C3L median, 1,415) stops calls in 14 C3L and 8 C3E units, moves the C3L − C3E difference from 7.3 to 9.1 points and still leaves C3L using 1.15 times the tokens of C3E. A cap is therefore either inert or a dev-tuned parameter that alters the primary comparison without equalizing cost. The token difference between C3L and C3E on dev (2,802 vs 2,036 per problem) comes from the trigger rate (25 vs 14 of 55 problems), not from the cost per triggered problem (about 3,580 vs 3,360 repair tokens); the comparison with equal trigger and calls is C3L vs C3T′ (H2), and C3A shows what more calls alone achieve. Units stopped by context limits are listed as deviations and excluded in a sensitivity analysis.

New conditions (qwen3:8b test only):

- **C3T′ (detection with generic content).** Trigger, active set and per-gene application counting are computed by the same function as C3L. Failures of the five protocol/execution genes in C3E's scope are rendered exactly as in C3L. Text from genes outside that scope never appears: their identifiers, details, repair instructions and soft warnings are omitted, and when at least one of them fails hard with applications left, one fixed generic OR review checklist is added instead. The checklist is the first k bullets of the existing C1 item pool, in pool order, with k chosen so that its length is within ±10% of the median length of the omitted content in C3L repair messages on the it1 dev run (about 530 characters); no bullet is selected by hand. It is identical across problems, hashed before freeze and not edited after rehearsal (registered 2026-10-04, SHA-256 a595f8c3607f98e81eefe93e9b200f5763c4ba8e1a3ca1d16cc1b4b6656078a9). Two of its four bullets (declare each decision variable as continuous, integer or binary; state the objective and its direction) restate the subject of two genes. They were found by the wording screen applied to the C1 pool after registration and were replaced in C1, but the registered checklist is kept unchanged. This makes C3T′ closer to C3L and H2 conservative. A C3T′ round-1 request therefore equals the C3L request exactly when no gene outside the generic-repair scope failed and no soft warning was raised.
- **C3A (every problem reviewed).** Identical to C3T′, except that problems with no hard failure at round 0 receive one forced review round containing the same checklist and the C3E closing sentence ("If the program is correct, return it unchanged"). If the reviewed program then fails a hard check, the remaining round is used as in C3T′. It measures what blanket review gains and how many correct programs it breaks.
- **C0R+E (rules in the prompt).** System prompt = C0 prompt + the contract compiled from the genes active for that problem under C3L (it1 text; the same `compile_contract` function and framing lines as C3, without the specification instruction); program only. Each gene's contract clauses are its prompt form. Repair instructions are written for a flagged program and are not placed in the prompt; this is stated as a limitation of the comparison. Repair uses the C3E template and trigger, at most 2 rounds. C0R@0 is the round-0 cut of the same run.
- **C3L-ALL** is not run. It is computed by offline replay: all genes' predicates are applied to the stored C3L programs, and the number of units whose trigger set would change is reported.

## 4. Confirmatory tests

- **H1 (primary).** C3L vs C3E, 2-round outcome, both benchmarks pooled. Exact two-sided McNemar test, α = 0.05; paired bootstrap 95% CI stratified by benchmark; problem-family cluster bootstrap CI as a sensitivity analysis. If H1 is not rejected, TOST with Δ = 5 percentage points (Tango score test) is reported and phrased as "any effect is smaller than 5 points" or "cannot be distinguished", never as "negligible" or "absent".
- **H2 (gated).** Tested only if H1 is rejected, at α = 0.05 (fixed-sequence gatekeeping; familywise error stays at 0.05). C3L vs C3T′ at the 1-round cut, where both conditions have the same trigger and the same number of calls. H2 is named "gene diagnosis and prescription beyond an equal-length generic checklist".
- Mechanism comparisons do not enter the Holm family of the other secondary comparisons. "Per-problem vs all activation" is removed from that family and reported descriptively.

## 5. Prespecified estimates and wording

### 5.1 Decomposition

Strata are defined only by round-0 offline checks, never by outcomes:
N = no hard failure; P = only protocol/execution genes fail; X = both kinds fail; M = only other genes fail.

At the 1-round cut, per problem: d(C3L − C3E) = d(C3T′ − C3E) + d(C3L − C3T′). Both terms are reported with point estimates and paired bootstrap CIs in the same resamples, together with wins, losses and broken-correct counts per stratum. The 2-round cut goes to the supplement. Net gains are also split by the function class of the genes actually applied in round 1 (protocol/execution, coding hygiene, OR modelling, answer-distribution heuristic).

### 5.2 Other estimates

- C3L vs C3A and C3L vs C0R+E, with mean calls and tokens per problem. C0R@0 accuracy and prompt length are reported descriptively; C0R@0 vs C1 is not used as evidence of knowledge specificity.
- gpt-oss:20b C3L vs C3E, reported as an out-of-sample test with respect to model programs: gene authors never saw gpt-oss programs. It does not test benchmark-content leakage.
- C3L − C3E per benchmark.
- Per-gene detection precision, recall and false-alarm rate on correct C0 programs (dev vs test round 0), by function class, with Wilson CIs.
- Detection–repair decomposition (descriptive), per condition (C3E, C3T′, C3L, C3A): among problems wrong at round 0, detection recall d (share flagged) and repair success s (share of flagged problems correct after repair); among problems correct at round 0, false-alarm rate and break rate. Final accuracy satisfies P_final = p0 + (1 − p0)·d·s − p0·f·b, where f is the false-alarm rate and b the break rate given a false alarm. Reported for test and, separately, for dev (in-sample). The two-term form of this identity appears in Yang et al. (2025, ACL); the gated form, stated for error rates, is Proposition 1 of Polat et al. (2026, arXiv:2607.17417); Tyen et al. (2024) measured error finding and correction separately. The decomposition is not claimed as a contribution.
- Generation seconds per correct answer, per condition.

### 5.3 Wording table (fixed before freeze)

- Any estimate: CI lower bound > 0 → "positive increment, estimate x [a, b]"; otherwise → "not distinguishable in this sample, CI [a, b]".
- H1 and H2 rejected → "gene diagnosis and prescription add accuracy beyond detection with generic advice"; the detection term is reported as an estimate.
- H1 rejected, H2 not rejected → if the detection term's CI lower bound > 0: "detection by gene predicates contributes; the additional contribution of diagnosis and prescription is [CI]"; otherwise: "this sample cannot separate the contributions of detection and prescription", with both estimates.
- "OR modelling knowledge" may be named as the source of the gain only if the X ∪ M strata, or gains attributed to OR-modelling genes, have a positive point estimate and account for more than half of the net gain. Otherwise the gain is described as "gene-guided repair of execution and coding failures".
- C3L vs C0R+E: if the C0R+E point estimate ≥ C3L, the claim becomes "rule content helps; an additional advantage of executable delivery was not detected".
- gpt-oss: CI lower bound > 0 → replicated; CI spanning 0 → not distinguishable; point estimate ≤ 0 → "not replicated", with both models' round-0 trigger rates.

## 6. Leakage and contamination safeguards

### 6.1 Library changes before freeze (it1)

Only two kinds of change, with no new genes and no change to predicate logic:

1. Clauses that name a specific problem type or family-specific technique are deleted or generalized. Known instances include "network throughput / net flow out of the source" (aggregates_declared_variables), "an item assigned to itself" and "minimise the largest of several totals" (nontrivial_optimum), and "a multiple of k" (linear_expressions_only). The rubric is written and hashed first. An isolated agent that does not write genes flags clauses and drafts generalized versions. For each flag the author may only delete it or adopt the agent's version; deletion is the default. Each decision is hashed and time-stamped before any dev replay. Replay results are disclosed and cannot trigger reversal or further editing.
2. Conservative deactivation rules for prompt-based C3 only, never stricter than the predicate's own precondition.

Before and after versions and per-problem dev differences go to the appendix.

### 6.2 Provenance and generality labels

- Provenance in three classes: seed_unrevised, seed_dev_revised, dev_derived. devCaseIds list every dev problem consulted when drafting or revising a version.
- The isolated agent labels each predicate's trigger condition as general or family-specific using the same rubric, and each gene's function class. Labels are registered before freeze.
- Predicates receive only the problem text (`{text}`); a unit test and an AST scan confirm that references and problem identifiers are unreachable.

### 6.3 Family stratification

- Problem-family labels: MAMO by frozen keyword classifier checked by an isolated agent; IndustryOR by the prespecified type table, assigned during the blind recomputation without seeing any outputs.
- Each successful repair is classified by whether the source family of the applied gene matches the problem's family: same-family, other-family, or seed-only.
- Wording, exhaustive with a default: other-family or cross-benchmark CI lower bound > 0 → "transfer across problem families"; same-family CI lower bound > 0 and other-family not → "gains were detectable only on problems of the genes' source families"; otherwise → "this sample cannot distinguish same-family from cross-family transfer".
- Conservative bound: for problems triggered at round 0 only by nontrivial_optimum, C3L's outcome is replaced by C3E's and H1's estimate and CI are recomputed. If the CI then spans 0, the paper states that part of the gain comes from an answer non-triviality check. An isolated agent reports, before freeze, how many test references are zero, infeasible or unbounded (counts only).

### 6.4 Contact register

An isolated script scans documents read by the author and drafting agents, and Claude/Codex session logs, and outputs only the test problem IDs found. These problems are analysed with and without exclusion. The 7 September v5 test output directory is recorded by SHA-256 to show it was not modified. Drafting-agent model IDs, browsing status and training cut-off dates are disclosed.

### 6.5 Registration and unsealing order

1. Register the it1 library, lexicon, predicate files, all templates (including the C3T′ checklist and C0R template), the test unit matrix and the analysis scripts: the freeze manifest (SHA-256 of every frozen file) together with this text (OSF version 1). The reference adjudication, the type labels and the exclusion list are not needed to run the test; they are appended as a time-stamped revision before any score is unsealed (step 4).
2. Run the gpt-oss dev20 rehearsal; an isolated agent keeps the outputs and reports only whether the protocol, parsing and logging passed.
3. Run the formal test once. Scores are sealed.
4. Recomputation, adjudication and family labels are completed by people who see no outputs and are appended to OSF with a timestamp.
5. Unseal scores.

### 6.6 Literal overlap audit

Longest common word n-grams, numeric constants (after placeholder substitution) and named entities between rendered repair messages and problems are reported in an appendix table with a dev positive control. The paper does not state that the audit rules out leakage.

### 6.7 Base-model contamination

Possible memorization by qwen3:8b may interact with repair (a check failure may prompt recall of a memorized solution); the paired design does not remove this. It is stated as a limitation and partly addressed by the gpt-oss and family-stratified results.

## 7. Schedule and determinism

All (problem, configuration) units are ordered by a recorded seed, interleaved and run in one window, with start times logged. Determinism is checked on dev without an extra run: the round-0 output of every dev problem was byte-identical in two runs on different days (55 of 55), and the 20 prespecified dev problems are run through the seeded schedule in the rehearsal and compared by content hash with the earlier fixed-order runs. A separate reverse-order rerun is not performed, because conditions that reuse round 0 cannot be placed before their source. These steps are reproducibility measures, not mechanism or anti-leakage evidence. After the test, round-0 agreement across conditions sharing it is checked and deviations are reported.

## 8. Rehearsal and dry run

New conditions (C3T′, C3A, C0R+E) are rehearsed on full dev55 with qwen3:8b, together with C0 as their shared round-0 source (campaign `d12-dev-qwen3-20261004`, run at the registered commit); results are used only for debugging, and the checklist and C0R template are not edited on the basis of them. Before freeze, all analysis scripts are run on dev outputs and the wording table is exercised on simulated scenarios.

## 9. Other secondary and exploratory analyses

Settings are those of `configs/prereg_analysis.json` (hashed at freeze). Each comparison is reported per model and per benchmark with case-level paired bootstrap 95% CIs and exact McNemar p-values; the case is the resampling unit.

- **Secondary comparisons, Holm-corrected within each family (α = 0.05), qwen3:8b, main analysis set, benchmarks pooled.** Ladder: C3E@2 vs C0; C3L@1 vs C3E@2; C3L@2 vs C3L@1; C2E@2 vs C2; C3@2 vs C3@0. Controls: C3L vs C0, C1, C2 and C3. gpt-oss:20b C3L vs C3E is a prespecified replication, reported separately and not in a Holm family (Section 5.2). C3 ablations (C3@0 − C2: contract content; C3@2 − C3@0: check and repair) are exploratory.
- **Length.** Mean input tokens, output tokens and calls per condition, for round 0 and in total. Primary model: conditional logistic regression correct ~ configuration + log(output tokens), one stratum per problem; sensitivity: GEE (binomial, exchangeable, clusters = problems). Robustness: accuracy per configuration within output-token quartiles pooled over the compared configurations.
- **Strata.** Problem scale: tertiles of the number of numeric literals in the problem statement (a measure of data volume that does not depend on model output; variable and constraint counts from solve records are missing where the C0 program fails). Cut points per benchmark from the dev problems of the frozen split (`analysis/r1/scale_features.py`): IndustryOR 12 and 18, MAMO 23.67 and 56.67; `data/features/scale_features.json` SHA-256 aa1a70e708e9000e7562a4b1d12b58fa5ac97e2c5039f79c7da4fe8a603097d8. IndustryOR difficulty (Easy/Medium/Hard). IndustryOR problem type from the adjudicated labels (Section 2). Accuracy, time-out rate and time are reported per stratum.
- **Sensitivity.** Second model; temperature 0.7 × 3 repeats on the 100-problem variance subset (qwen3:8b only; per-problem mean over repeats before resampling); three tolerances (1e-6, 1e-4, 1e-2, absolute and relative); MAMO all 211; original references instead of adjudicated ones; including the problems viewed on 2026-09-05 and contact-register problems; well-specified subset. Summarised in one table and forest plot. The variance subset (100 test problems: 53 MAMO, 47 IndustryOR) and the determinism set (20 dev problems: 11 MAMO, 9 IndustryOR; Section 7, also used for the 20-problem rehearsals) are drawn from the main-analysis split locks by seed 20261004, proportionally per benchmark, taking the first n problem IDs in ascending order of sha256("seed|subset|id") (`tools/draw_subsets_r1.py`; only ID files are read). The IDs are in `data/subsets_r1/variance_determinism_ids.json`, SHA-256 faf737de90dc78b5bc0fa7bdb213e8fa790228667d364a2b81414f8c5506aec0, listed in the registration. Variance-subset problems later reported separately as non-linearizable stay in the subset and are analysed with that group. The main analysis always uses the first run.
- **Order effects.** Regression of correctness on run position under the recorded random schedule.
- **Activation and conflicts.** Per-problem activation rate; C3L-ALL offline replay (Section 3); conflict statistics per gene pair and the number of problems not converged after two rounds.
- **Execution and false reports.** Truncation rate, execution-failure types, solver-status distribution, errors classified by gene predicate, and the false-report rate (reported status or objective that the solver record does not support).
- **If C3 underperforms C0.** Three explanations are checked: (i) reasoning degrades under extra format requirements; (ii) the contract crowds out the generation budget; (iii) repair fixes execution errors but not modelling errors. Evidence: round-0 failure types by predicate, output length and truncation, and whether the pattern repeats on gpt-oss:20b.
- **Fallback for the claim.** If H1 is not rejected, the claim is rewritten from C3L vs C0 and C3E vs C0 ("executable checks with bounded repair improve accuracy, but gene-targeted repair was not shown to outperform generic repair"); C2/C3 vs C0/C1 differences are secondary only; if neither holds, no difference is reported with TOST (Section 4).
- Any analysis not listed here is labelled post hoc.

## 10. Measurement check

See `stage1/measurement_check/06_prereg_measurement_check_EN.md`. Only C0, C3E and C3L outputs are judged; C3T′, C3A and C0R+E outputs are not added. After judging the programs and settling their disagreements, each checker answers two questions for every gene of the it1 library, independently: is the principle correct (yes / no / unsure), and is it general or specific to one kind of problem. The answers are reported per gene and per checker as they are, as the external check of the encoded preferences; no agreement coefficient is computed (with 21 genes and two raters its standard deviation is 0.20–0.35 in simulation, and 60–110 items would be needed for a standard error of 0.10). Obvious errors they point out are listed in the appendix; the test library is not changed. Each gene's generality and function class are also labelled by the isolated agent before freeze (Section 6.2).

## 11. Deviations

Any deviation after registration is dated, justified and reported; analyses outside this plan are labelled post hoc.
